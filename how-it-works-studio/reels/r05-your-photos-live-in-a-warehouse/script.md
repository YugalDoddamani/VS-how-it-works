**The promise of this reel:** You will be able to picture the building, the copies, and the power bill behind one photo.

**What the viewer keeps:** Nothing digital is weightless. Your photos are in a warehouse, copied, cooled and paid for.

## Hooks (test all three, keep the winner)

1. "There is a city with no people in it. Your photos live there." **← in shot 1**
2. "The cloud is not in the sky. It is a building with a security guard."
3. "Your holiday is in a windowless building, three times over."

Render each as a 4-second cold open and compare 3-second retention. Only the
first four seconds change; the rest of the reel is identical.

## The look

Casual: pink tee, charcoal shorts, hair loose, sitting on the floor with her back to the sofa. Phone in hand for the first two shots, then she is on her feet and presenting. cosiest grade of the five.

## Key shots in detail

**Shot 1.** The scroll has to feel real: 20+ thumbnails flying past in 42 frames, some blurred. It is the only shot with genuine speed, and it sets up the contrast with the slow warehouse.

**Shot 3.** The thumbnail lifting off the screen is the transition from 'your phone' to 'the world'. One card, 18 frames, arc up and right, then cut.

**Shot 5 (CORNER).** The warehouse assembles from the floor up — walls, roof, racks — over 40 frames. This is the reveal, so it gets the most build time of any shot in the reel.

**Shot 9.** The three-copy move is the saveable frame: one card becomes three, each sliding to a different building in a different direction. Hold long enough to count them.

**Shot 15.** 1.5% is the only number in the reel. It gets its own frame, nothing else on screen, and no animation on the card itself — the stillness makes it a fact rather than a graphic.

**Shots 19–25.** The wonder has to be paid off with a cost, so the block moves from resilience (one building can vanish) to the bill, then to the one thing she actually wants the viewer to do. Shot 25, thumb on the backup confirmation, is the callback to shot 1: same screen, opposite mood.

**Shot 27.** She is back where she started, with the phone gone and her hands free. The loop closes visually even though the reel is not a loop.

## How she talks to the viewer

| Device | Where it is used |
| --- | --- |
| She starts as the viewer | Shots 1–3: she is scrolling her own camera roll, in the dark, exactly like the viewer. She only turns presenter after the reveal. |
| Direct address | She is on screen in 18 of 27 shots, and looks down the lens on every 'you'. |
| Hand-trailing walk | Shot 12: she walks the warehouse and drags a hand along a rack — motion that sells scale and keeps a long explanation alive. |
| Counting with fingers | Shot 9: three copies counted physically on three buildings, not listed in text. |
| One number | Shot 15 holds a single stat with no competition on screen. Explainers are remembered for one number, not five. |
| Comment prompt | Caption only: 'how many photos are in your camera roll?' — a number people like to share. |

## Sound

No music for the first 3 shots — only the tiny scroll ticks of a camera roll. A low, warm swelling pad enters as the warehouse assembles (shot 5) and carries the reel. Copy duplicates get three soft 'chimes', rising in pitch. The 1.5% card lands in near silence, and the recap restores the pad for the sign-off.
