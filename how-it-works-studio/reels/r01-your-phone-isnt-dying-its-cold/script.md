# R01 — Your phone isn't dying. It's cold.

> Why does my phone die the second I step outside?

| Shots | 26 · average 1.86 s per shot |
| Outfit | `hoodie` — Winter, outside, hands in pockets. The hoodie is what she is actually wearing in this weather. |

**The promise of this reel:** By the end you know why the number falls, why it comes back, and the one pocket that fixes it.

**What the viewer keeps:** You are not being scammed by your phone. Cold slows a chemical reaction. That is all.

## Hooks (test all three, keep the winner)

1. "Your phone is at 40%. You step outside. It dies." **← in shot 1**
2. "Your phone did not die. It got cold."
3. "That 40% never left. It just stopped being able to leave."

Render each as a 4-second cold open and compare 3-second retention. Only the
first four seconds change; the rest of the reel is identical.

## The look

Black oversized hoodie, sleeves pushed to the elbow so her hands read. Hair slightly flattened by a beanie that sits off-frame — implied, never seen. Breath fog in the outside shots, which is the cheapest cold signal there is.

## Key shots in detail

**Shot 1.** The whole reel lives or dies here. Hold the 40% for one full beat before any motion, so the viewer reads the number. No music yet — the badge appears on a hard cut with a single tick. Start the VO on frame 4, not frame 0: a half-second of silence makes the number feel like a fact.

**Shot 3 → 4.** The whip from her hand to the screen is the fastest cut in the reel (12 frames of blur, then a 6-frame settle). This is the moment the hook lands, so it gets the only whip in the reel.

**Shot 6 (CORNER).** First corner-overlay shot, and it establishes the grammar: she is keyed bottom-right at 34% height, the iso cutaway fills the frame behind her, and the caption band stays clear at the bottom-left. The overlay never covers the caption.

**Shot 9 (SPLIT).** The hard vertical edge at 62% is deliberate — no soft gradient. Warm side gets a 1px warm outline, cold side a cold one. The toggle flips at the exact word 'slow', not before.

**Shot 18.** Three props assemble in stagger order while she lists them: pocket, warm room, charger. Each chip lands on a word, 8 frames apart. Do not animate the chips after they land.

**Shots 21–24.** The damage question is the one every viewer is actually asking, and it arrives *after* the mechanism, so it reads as a fair follow-up rather than a caveat bolted on. Shot 22 (two phones, one in a scarf) is the only place two objects share the frame — keep the scarf phone slightly warmer in grade so the comparison lands without a label.

**Shot 26.** The last shot is the only one where she is centred and still. Everything before it is moving. The stillness is what makes the sign-off feel like a person, not a loop.

## How she talks to the viewer

| Device | Where it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Direct address | She is on screen in 17 of 26 shots, and looks down the lens on every 'you'. |
| Point at camera | Shot 13: finger enters frame and points at the viewer, breaking the fourth wall at the exact moment of the promise. |
| Hand in frame | Shots 3, 6, 12, 19: her hand enters to hold, warm, or block the object. The hand is what makes it feel filmed rather than rendered. |
| Question → answer | The VO asks nothing directly, but shot 2 ends on 'And it dies.' — a statement the viewer has already experienced. Recognition beats a question for this topic. |
| Callback | Shot 24 reuses the charger prop from shot 18 so the warning lands on something already seen. |
| Comment prompt | In the caption only: 'which pocket are you?' Never spoken, never a sticker pointing down at the UI. |

## Sound

One low tick on the 40% badge. No music bed until shot 3. From shot 3 a cold, sparse pulse at 92 BPM — deliberately slower than the cut rate, so the edits feel fast while the music stays calm. A soft glass 'ting' on the number crash (shot 4), a warm swelling pad under shot 12–15 when the charge returns, and total silence for the 8 frames before the recap chips.

## Production notes

Outfit: hoodie. Grade: warm interior 3200K, cold exterior 5600K with a blue lift in the shadows — the grade does half the storytelling. Never show a real phone brand or OS chrome. The battery cutaway is invented geometry: two electrodes, a liquid, and dots. Do not attempt chemistry accuracy beyond 'ions move slower'.
