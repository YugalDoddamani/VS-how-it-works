**The promise of this reel:** You will know which cookie does it, why it can follow you across sites, and the one switch that stops it.

**What the viewer keeps:** The cookie is not evil. The tracker that is allowed to be on every site is.

## Hooks (test all three, keep the winner)

1. "You looked at shoes once. The shoes are everywhere now." **← in shot 1**
2. "You are not being followed. You are being recognised. There is a difference and it is worse."
3. "One tiny file is why that sofa is stalking you."

Render each as a 4-second cold open and compare 3-second retention. Only the
first four seconds change; the rest of the reel is identical.

## The look

Casual dress: off-shoulder knit, pink plaid skirt, hair tucked behind one ear. Shopping-bag prop in shot 1, phone in shot 3. Brighter grade than the other reels — this one is allowed to feel like a Saturday.

## Key shots in detail

**Shot 1 → 2.** The match cut is the hook: the same shoe card, same position, different surroundings. Cut on the identical framing and let the ad badge appear on frame 4 of shot 2. Recognition is instant and requires no explanation.

**Shot 3.** Her first line is a denial — 'that is not reading your mind' — which is the most engaging possible opening after a hook, because the viewer's theory is being taken seriously before it is replaced.

**Shot 5 (CORNER).** The shelf is the metaphor. It must be visually identical in shots 5 and 9 so the cross-site move reads as the same box travelling. Do not redesign it between shots.

**Shot 9.** The box slides on a dotted arc between shelves, 18 frames, ease-in-out. It is the single most important motion in the reel: this is the explanation, rendered as an object moving.

**Shot 14.** The spec card is deliberately unglamorous — paper, small type, no animation. It is the credibility beat, and credibility should not be pretty.

**Shots 19–25.** The number-not-name beat is what makes the explanation click: the note never needs her identity, only continuity. Shot 22 is the incognito correction, which is the comment-section fight this reel exists to pre-empt. Keep the incognito window dimmed and inert — it is a dead end, and it should look like one.

**Shot 27.** Last line is the rule in one sentence. She does not point at anything; the line does the work. Cut 6 frames after she stops speaking.

## How she talks to the viewer

| Device | Where it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Direct address | She is on screen in 18 of 27 shots, and looks down the lens on every 'you'. |
| Object handed to camera | Shots 4, 5, 9: the cookie is a physical prop she picks up and passes toward the lens. |
| Agree, then replace | Shot 3 grants the viewer's theory ('not reading your mind') before correcting it — the least defensive way to correct anyone. |
| Measured with hands | Shot 11: hands apart to show 'it does not need your name, just the box'. Physical scale for an abstract idea. |
| One-switch payoff | Shot 17 goes to settings and flips the switch on camera. The viewer can copy it before the reel ends. |
| Comment prompt | Caption only: 'what is the ad that found you?' — story bait, and it doubles as topic research. |

## Sound

Bright, light percussion at 104 BPM from shot 2. A soft 'pop' every time the cookie box lands on a shelf. The spec-card beat drops to a single held tone — the one moment the reel gets serious. Settings switch gets a satisfying click, and the recap chips land on four ticks.
