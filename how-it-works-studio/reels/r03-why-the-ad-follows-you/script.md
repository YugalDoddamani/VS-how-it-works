# R03 — Why the ad follows you around.

> How does an ad know I looked at that?

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Series | **Your Data, Explained** |
| Length | **45.0 s** · 1350 frames @ 30 fps |
| Shots | 23 · average 1.96 s per shot |
| Outfit | `casual-dress` — Off-shoulder top and pink plaid skirt: the reel is about shopping, so she is dressed for the part. |
| Cut rate | one cut every 2.0 s (hook cuts land at 1.2–1.6 s) |
| Status | **scripted** — not rendered |

**The promise of this reel:** You will know which cookie does it, why it can follow you across sites, and the one switch that stops it.

**What the viewer keeps:** The cookie is not evil. The tracker that is allowed to be on every site is.

**The 45-second shape.** 0–4.2 s recognition (3 shots) · 4.2–10.4 s the world arrives (3) ·
10.4–26.6 s mechanism (8) · 26.6–38.8 s credibility and the switch (6) · 38.8 s the save
frame and the recap (2) · 43.0 s her, for two seconds, and out (1). Every second is
carrying a beat; nothing is padding and nothing is a second helping.

## Hooks (test all three, keep the winner)

1. "You looked at shoes once. The shoes are everywhere now." **← in shot 1**
2. "You are not being followed. You are being recognised. There is a difference and it is worse."
3. "One tiny file is why that sofa is stalking you."

Render each as a 4.2-second cold open and compare 3-second retention. Only shots
1–3 change; the rest of the reel is identical.

**Hook rhythm at 45 s.** Shot 1 holds 1.4 s on the shoe listing — long enough to
read the price, short enough that the thumb is still moving when the cut lands.
Shot 2 does the recognition work on a match cut (identical framing, new
surroundings). Shot 3 is the first time she appears, and she appears disagreeing.

## The look

Casual dress: off-shoulder knit, pink plaid skirt, hair tucked behind one ear. Shopping-bag prop in shot 1, phone in shot 3. Brighter grade than the other reels — this one is allowed to feel like a Saturday.

The world is a **shelf system**, not a warehouse: small tagged boxes on small shelves, one shelf per site. The shelf must be built once and reused in shots 5, 8, 10 and 21 — the whole explanation depends on it being the same object in the same place.

## Key shots in detail

**Shots 1 → 2.** The match cut is the hook and it has to be pixel-identical in
framing: same shoe card, same position in frame, different surroundings. Cut on
the identical composition and let the ad badge appear on frame 4 of shot 2.
Recognition needs no explanation — that is why this is the cheapest hook in the
slate.

**Shot 3.** Her first line is a denial — 'that is not reading your mind' — which
is the most engaging possible opening after a hook, because the viewer's theory is
taken seriously before it is replaced. This is also the only whip in the reel:
90° out of the phone screen, 10 frames of blur, 6 frames of settle.

**Shot 5 (CORNER).** The shelf is the metaphor. Shot 5 builds it, shot 8 moves a
box between two of them, shot 10 shows the two shelves linked, shot 21 shows all
of it flat at once. Never redesign the shelf between those four shots.

**Shot 8.** The box slides on a dotted arc between shelves, 18 frames,
ease-in-out. This is the explanation rendered as an object moving, so it gets the
longest single motion in the reel.

**Shot 12.** The number-not-name beat is what makes the whole thing click: the
note never needs her identity, only continuity. Hold the flat note for the full
2.4 s with one slow 3 % push — no other motion, and nothing else on screen.

**Shot 15.** The spec card is deliberately unglamorous: paper, small type, no
animation. It is the credibility beat and credibility should not be pretty. Sound
drops to a single held tone here.

**Shots 18 → 20.** The switch is flipped on camera in 18, the cost is admitted in
19, and 20 holds the finger on the toggle for 2.0 s. That held frame is the
second-most saveable image in the reel and the one that makes the fix feel small
enough to actually do.

**Shot 21.** The save frame. Everything else leaves the screen: one flat isometric
grid, the shelf, both sites, the switch, and one line of text. Hold 2.4 s with
near-silence under it. This is the frame that gets screenshotted, so it must work
as a still with the sound off and no context.

**Shot 23.** The last line is the rule in one sentence. She does not point at
anything; the line does the work. Cut 6 frames after she stops speaking.

## How she talks to the viewer

| Device | Where it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Direct address | She is on screen in 13 of 23 shots, and looks down the lens on every 'you'. |
| Agree, then replace | Shot 3 grants the viewer's theory ('not reading your mind') before correcting it — the least defensive way to correct anyone. |
| Object handed to camera | Shots 4, 5, 6: the cookie is a physical prop she picks up and passes toward the lens. |
| Measured with hands | Shot 4: one finger for 'one small file', then the box at chest height. Physical scale for an abstract idea. |
| One-switch payoff | Shots 18 and 20: she flips the switch on camera and holds her finger there. The viewer can copy it before the reel ends. |
| Honest cost | Shot 19: the price of the fix, said out loud, before the viewer discovers it and feels tricked. |
| Comment prompt | Caption only: 'what is the ad that found you?' — story bait, and it doubles as topic research. |

**Direct-address count: 13 of 23.** That is higher than the other reels on
purpose. This topic is about the viewer's own device, so the reel is mostly her
talking to a person, with the shelf doing the diagramming behind her.

**Ten cards say 'you'.** Six land on her face (3, 5, 13, 14, 18, 23) and four
land on the viewer's own hands (1, 12, 17, 20). That split is deliberate: the
first half of the reel is about her stuff, the second half is about yours, and a
hand in frame is a stronger 'you' than a face when the subject is a phone screen.

## Production notes

Outfit: casual-dress. Shopping UI is invented — no real retailer, no real ad
network. The 'logo stand-in' on the cookie is a generic shape, never a real
company mark. The incognito window is invented chrome, dimmed, with no readable
text. Brighter grade than the other reels; keep the paper background and the
orange accent so it still reads as the channel.

**Props needed, and only these:** one phone, one shopping bag, one small tagged
box (hero prop, shot 4 onward), one paper spec card, one generic stamp.

**What the 45-second cut drops, and why.** The earlier 54-second draft had a
separate "why you see the shoes and not the exact pair" shot, a "sites trade the
number" shot, and a fourth check in the settings panel. All three restated beats
that shots 12, 8 and 18 already carry, and together they were nine seconds of
second helpings. Dropping them is what buys the 2.4 s save frame at shot 21,
which the longer cut did not have room for.
