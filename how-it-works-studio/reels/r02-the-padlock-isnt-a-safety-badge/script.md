# R02 — The padlock isn't a safety badge.

> Does the padlock in my browser mean the site is safe?

**The promise of this reel:** You will know exactly what the padlock proves, what it does not, and the one thing to read instead.

**What the viewer keeps:** HTTPS locks the tunnel. It does not check who built the house at the end of it.

## Hooks (test all three, keep the winner)

1. "That little padlock is not telling you what you think." **← in shot 1**
2. "The padlock means nobody can read your message. It does not mean the person is honest."
3. "Scammers have the padlock too. It is free and takes four minutes."

Render each as a 4-second cold open and compare 3-second retention. Only the
first four seconds change; the rest of the reel is identical.

## The look

Office casual: white shirt, dark pencil skirt, lanyard. Sitting at a desk in the first shot, standing by the third. A phone in hand for the scam-message beat, a laptop for the address-bar beat.

## Key shots in detail

**Shot 1.** Open on the message, not on her. The viewer has to read the scam before she comments on it, otherwise the joke lands before the setup. The message is on screen for 1.4s — long enough to read eight words.

**Shot 3.** The 3× punch-in on the padlock is a single scale animation over 10 frames, then hold. No bounce. This is the object the reel is about, so it gets one deliberate push and then stillness.

**Shot 4.** The hard 'No.' is the reel's first laugh and its first cut on a single syllable. Cut 2 frames after the mouth closes, not on the word — it reads as decisive rather than clipped.

**Shot 6 (CORNER).** The tunnel is the visual metaphor that carries the whole reel, so its first appearance gets the longest shot. Build it: two walls, a floor, and a seal that snaps shut in 6 frames.

**Shot 10 (SPLIT).** The unmarked building must look *nice*. If it looks sinister the point is lost — the danger is that it looks normal.

**Shots 18–24.** This block is the reel's actual product: three checks the viewer can run in five seconds. Each check gets its own shot and its own `INSERT` or `CORNER`, never two checks in one frame. Shot 22 is the concession — the lock really does protect you on café wifi — and it must be played straight, because a reel that only subtracts from the padlock is a reel that gets argued with in the comments.

**Shot 13.** The lookalike domain highlight is the single most saveable frame in the reel. Hold it 72 frames and put nothing else on screen. This is the frame people screenshot.

## How she talks to the viewer

| Device | Where it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Direct address | She is on screen in 19 of 26 shots, and looks down the lens on every 'you'. |
| Reader as co-conspirator | Shot 2: she reads the message, then flicks her eyes to the lens. The viewer is standing next to her. |
| Object handed to camera | Shot 3 and 14: the address bar is pushed toward the viewer as if to say 'look at this'. |
| Answering the obvious question | Shot 3 asks the viewer's question in her voice ('So it must be safe, right?') and shot 4 answers 'No.' — the fastest possible agreement-then-turn. |
| Repeatable test | Shot 16 turns the explanation into three checks the viewer can run in five seconds on the next scam text. |
| Comment prompt | Caption only: 'what is the sketchiest message you have got?' — it invites a story, not a like. |

## Sound

Keyboard click on shot 1, message whoosh on shot 3, a single low 'thunk' as the tunnel seals (shot 6), then a tight, dry beat — no melody — until the recap, where the pad drops out entirely for 24 frames. The 'No.' in shot 4 lands in a 4-frame pocket of silence.
