**The promise of this reel:** You will know why it is always the same time, and the three fixes that actually work.

**What the viewer keeps:** Shared air, a shared pipe, everyone at once. It is a queue, not a curse.

## Hooks (test all three, keep the winner)

1. "Nine pm. Every night. It is not a coincidence." **← in shot 1**
2. "Your wifi is not broken. Your street is watching a film."
3. "There are only three lanes and everyone got home at once."

Render each as a 4-second cold open and compare 3-second retention. Only the
first four seconds change; the rest of the reel is identical.

## The look

Sporty: black crop top, black shorts, hair tied back, a towel over one shoulder. She is mid-reel workout, which is why the first cut is a dropped stream. Trainer on the floor, water bottle prop.

## Key shots in detail

**Shot 1.** Open on the buffering spinner, not on her. Everyone has watched that spinner. One second of it does more than any line of script.

**Shot 2.** The cut from tablet to her face is a 90° whip, 10 frames of blur. She is mid-workout and out of breath, which is why the reel feels fast: she is moving before the edit is.

**Shot 8 (SPLIT).** The three-lane diagram is the saveable frame. Three coloured lanes with the gaps labelled — nothing else on screen. Hold 72 frames.

**Shot 10.** The street-at-night shot is the emotional payoff: every window lighting up in sequence, 4 frames apart, then all streams starting at once. This is the moment the viewer says 'oh'.

**Shot 16.** The cable is a real physical action, not an animation: she plugs it in, the icon changes, the spinner stops. Show the *result*, not just the instruction.

**Shots 19–24.** The "restarting sometimes does nothing" moment from shot 14 is paid off here, which is why this block exists at all: the viewer who has already tried the router needs to be told why it failed before they will try a cable. Shot 23 (the clock with a queue under it) is the diagnosis test in one image.

**Shot 26.** The sign-off is the only relaxed shot. She laughs at herself, which is what keeps a lecture about routers from feeling like a lecture.

## How she talks to the viewer

| Device | Where it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Relatable failure first | Shot 1 is the viewer's own evening. No introduction, no logo, no 'hey guys'. |
| Direct address | She is on screen in 18 of 26 shots, and looks down the lens on every 'you'. |
| Counting on fingers | Shots 7 and 15: the three fixes are physically counted, so the viewer can hold them in memory. |
| Hand-in-frame demonstrations | Shot 16 plugs a real cable in front of the lens; shot 18 walks toward the router so distance is shown, not described. |
| Two-distance demo | Shot 13: hands apart for 'last few metres' vs 'the long part' — the distinction most people are missing. |
| Comment prompt | Caption only: 'which fix did you try first?' — invites a reply that is also useful data. |

## Sound

No music until shot 5. From there a light, percussive 108 BPM track that drops out completely under shot 11 (the pipe) so the queue feels crowded. Buffering spinner has a dry ticking noise that stops dead on shot 16's click. The night street is silent except for a low room hum.
