# Motion and Timing

## Motion philosophy

Motion has one job here: **point at what to look at next**, so the viewer's eye
and the narrator's sentence arrive together.

An explainer is dense. If the world moves constantly, the viewer reads neither
the caption nor the props. So the rule is: *hold, then move, then hold*.

- The background world is mostly still. Props assemble during the first third of
  a beat and then hold.
- Camera moves happen on beat changes, not during a sentence.
- The caption band is never animated while it is legible. Words fade in over the
  first 6–10 frames and then nothing moves until the card leaves.

All motion is a pure function of `(episode, frame)`. No clocks, no
`Math.random()`, no CSS animations, no network.

## Timing defaults

- 30 fps.
- 45–75 s target; 90 s hard ceiling.
- At 30 fps: 0.2 s = 6 f, 0.5 s = 15 f, 1 s = 30 f, 2 s = 60 f.
- Every duration in the sheet is authored in seconds, converted at compile time.
  The last beat absorbs the rounding remainder so the total is exact.

## Where the time in a beat goes

A 7-second beat (210 frames) has a shape, and it is nearly always this one:

| Phase | Frames | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| arrive | 0–18 | caption fades in (word-staggered), Nao moves to the pose |
| build | 18–70 | props drop-and-settle, staggered 4 f apart |
| **hold** | 70–150 | **nothing moves** except blink, breathe, and one emphasis underline |
| accent | 150–185 | the one thing being talked about gets its accent outline, a label pins on |
| settle | 185–210 | hand-off: props hold, Nao returns toward neutral, caption leaves |

That `hold` is not laziness, it is the design. It is where the viewer reads.

Rules that follow from it:
- No beat shorter than 3 s (90 f). Under that there is no hold.
- No beat longer than 12 s (360 f) without a second movement event.
- The `accent` object must be on screen for at least 45 f, or the viewer misses
  what the sentence was about.

## Standard motions

All names below are `events[]` types the stage understands; a beat's implicit
motions are generated by its `beat_type`, and only overrides belong in the sheet.

### Caption

- Fade in, **word-staggered**, over `min(40, words × 4)` frames.
- Words keep their layout slot from frame 0, so the line does not reflow while
  it is being read — only opacity changes. This is the single most important
  motion decision in the project.
- Emphasis tokens fade in 4 frames *after* their neighbours and get a 1 px
  underline that grows left-to-right over 8 frames. No scale, no bounce.
- Exit: 6-frame fade, or a hard cut on a beat boundary. Never slide.

### Prop assembly

- `drop-and-settle`, 12 f, as specified in `05-isometric-world.md`.
- Staggered 4 f per prop, in slot order (front row first).
- This is the *only* way a prop enters. Rarity makes it readable.

### Camera

- Preset-to-preset: 14 f, `easeInOutCubic`.
- Focus interpolates in world space.
- A zoom-in ends with a 20 f hold before the label pins on, so the label does not
  arrive during the move.

### Label pin

- The pin line draws from the prop's anchor to the label over 10 f, then the
  label fades in over 6 f.
- The label holds for the rest of the beat. Labels never move once placed.

### Nao

- Pose change: 12 f, `easeInOutCubic`, on all joint angles together.
- Gesture: additive, per the gesture's own length, `easeOutCubic` in and
  `easeInOutCubic` out.
- Blink: 4 f (2 close, 2 open), seeded.
- Breathe: 4 s sine, ±1.5 px.
- She never moves during the `hold` phase beyond idle life, and never enters or
  leaves frame mid-episode — except on `hook` (enters) and `outro` (stays).

### Emphasis

- Accent outline: 8 f fade of a 1 px stroke.
- Underline: 8 f left-to-right grow.
- Never a colour flash, never a pulse, never a shake.

### Transitions

| Type | Behaviour | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fade` | 10 f cross-fade, previous beat held underneath | default |
| `wipe` | 12 f vertical card wipe, edge aligned to the iso grid | between sections |
| `none` | hard cut | inside `step-by-step`, `compare` |

No spin, no zoom-blur, no glitch. This is a calm channel.

### Cues: motion that follows the sentence

A beat can carry a **cue list** (`src/stage/reel/cues.js`): a handful of anchors,
each naming the word in the narration at which the picture advances, plus the
note the viewer should read on the prop while that state is live.

- The word's share of the read is proportional to its length, not even, so the
  tenth word lands where the voice says it.
- A cue runs until the next cue starts. A shot cut in the middle of one resumes
  the state it left, so a three-shot beat is one continuous demonstration seen
  from three angles rather than the same loop three times.
- Every cue word is checked against the narration at compile time. Renaming a
  word in the script fails the build instead of letting the picture drift a
  second out of sync.

A painter reads `o.cue.index` (which state the sentence has reached) and
`o.beatT` (progress through the beat, not the shot) instead of a per-shot ramp.
That is the whole difference between a background that moves and a picture that
explains.

### Rings and reaches

Painters report where the thing they are currently demonstrating sits, with
`o.focus(x, y, r)`. The frame then:

- rings it, with four lens ticks that read as a camera rather than a bubble;
- letters its cue note above the ring, in small caps, with no pill behind it;
- and, if she is in the frame, draws a short dashed reach from her extended hand
  toward it, ending in a pointer tick.

The reach is capped at about 300 px and fades with distance: a gesture has a
length, and a line drawn all the way to a prop at the top of the frame stops
being a gesture and becomes a plumb line through the picture. Her body also
leans a little toward the ring, which is where the eye goes anyway.

## Pacing by level

The `level` column picks a preset. These are starting numbers, not laws.

| Level | Beat length | Words/caption | Props/beat | Camera |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `beginner` | 6–9 s | 10–14 | 1–3 | holds, few zoom changes |
| `intermediate` | 5–7 s | 12–16 | 2–4 | zoom-ins allowed |
| `advanced` | 4–6 s | 14–18 | 3–5 | more `diagram`, faster holds |

A `beginner` episode that renders at 60 s with 10 beats is closer to correct than
a dense 12-beat version. When in doubt, be slower and shorter.

## Reading-speed verification

Caption hold time is computed, not guessed:

```
needed_frames = round((words / 4 + 0.6) * 30)
```

`npm run check` warns when a caption's `durationFrames` is under that. It cannot
detect a *boring* hold, only a rushed one — and a rushed one is the failure that
silently loses viewers, because nobody rewinds an explainer to read a caption.

## Determinism rules

- Time comes from the frame number: `t = frame / fps`.
- Randomness comes from `Motion.mulberry32(Motion.hashString(seed))`, seeded from
  `beat_id` — so blinks, the packet's tail jitter, and any particle field are
  reproducible.
- No `Date.now()`, `performance.now()`, `Math.random()`, CSS `@keyframes`,
  `requestAnimationFrame`-driven state, or `setTimeout` in `src/stage/`.
- Fonts load before the first capture; the stage exposes a `ready` promise.
- `npm run determinism -- <slug>` renders twice and compares bytes. It is the
  test that catches every one of the violations above.

## What good looks like

Watch an episode muted, once, without pausing:

- At any moment you can tell what you are supposed to look at.
- The caption was always on screen before you needed it.
- Nothing moved while you were reading.
- The world grew, and you could see the answer taking shape.

If any of those fail, the fix is usually to remove motion, not add it.
